The SIE file — why it changes the engagement

SIE (Standard Import Export) has been Sweden's accounting interchange standard since 1992, created by a non-profit group of Swedish software vendors and professional bodies. Effectively every accounting package sold in Sweden supports it.

SIE4 — the file we ask for first

Full transactions and vouchers for the fiscal year. A complete audit trail, not a period summary. This is the single most useful file on a Swedish target and almost every ledger system can produce it.

SIE5 — the XML revision

Released in 2018 to make the standard internationally readable. We handle it where the target's system emits it, though SIE4 remains far more common in practice.

SIE1–SIE3 — the summary types

Year-end balances, period balances and object balances respectively. Useful for a quick orientation, but they cannot support voucher-level diligence questions.

What that means in practice

On many offshore engagements, week one goes to rebuilding a trial balance from whatever the seller could export. A Swedish target should not need that step. One SIE4 file carries the monthly P&L, balance sheet, account-level movement and voucher-level support in a single structured export.

The practical consequences: the seller's controller is not tied up producing custom extracts, more of your budget goes to analysis rather than data entry, and when a buyer later asks “what sits behind this cost line?” the answer is already in the file rather than requiring a fresh request.

It also means our first-cut databook lands faster on a Swedish deal than on a comparable UK or US mandate.

K2, K3 and IFRS — where the differences bite

A buyer underwriting on an IFRS or K3 basis cannot read K2 numbers at face value. These are the divergences that actually move EBITDA and net debt.

Which standard applies

Every Swedish limited company reports under K2, K3 or IFRS. K2 is the simplified regime for smaller companies; K3 is the main standard; IFRS applies to listed groups. Most mid-market targets you look at will be K2 or K3.

Capitalised development costs

K3 permits capitalisation of development costs in consolidated accounts where the criteria are met, but not in single-entity statements. K2 prohibits it outright. On a software or product target this single difference can move reported EBITDA materially.

Goodwill

K3 requires goodwill to be amortised — unlike IFRS, where it is tested for impairment. A K3 target will therefore show an amortisation charge that an IFRS-basis buyer will normally add back.

P&L format

K3 allows either cost-by-function or cost-by-nature presentation; K2 permits only one. Comparing two Swedish targets often means restating one of them before the numbers mean anything side by side.

Leases

K3 treatment differs from IFRS 16. Operating leases that a buyer would capitalise under IFRS sit off balance sheet under K3, which affects both EBITDA and net debt.

Why it matters in diligence

A buyer underwriting on an IFRS or K3 basis cannot read K2 numbers at face value. We bridge reported figures onto the basis you are underwriting, and show the bridge rather than just the answer.

Statutory reporting

Swedish limited companies file an årsredovisning with Bolagsverket. We reconcile those filed statutory accounts back to management reporting as a standard early step, because that reconciliation is almost always where a buyer's first substantive questions land — particularly where the statutory accounts are prepared on a different basis or with different cut-offs from the monthly management pack.

Where a target has an auditor, we work alongside them rather than around them.

The Swedish M&A market

  • The Nordics generate roughly 12–15% of European private equity deal volume by count, from a combined population of about 27 million — a persistently disproportionate share.
  • Sweden is the most active Nordic PE market, underpinned by technology, industrials, healthcare, consumer brands and export-oriented mid-market companies.
  • Stockholm hosts EQT (the largest Nordic-headquartered investor by enterprise value) and Nordic Capital, alongside mid-market houses including IK Partners, Adelis, Segulah and Axcel.
  • Nordic Capital closed its second dedicated mid-market fund, Evolution II, at a €2bn hard cap in December 2024 — capital specifically targeted at the deal size where outsourced diligence is common.
  • A deep base of owner-led businesses reaching succession means consistent lower-mid-market transaction flow, which is exactly where in-house diligence teams do not exist.
