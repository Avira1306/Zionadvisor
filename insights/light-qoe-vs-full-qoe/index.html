Light QoE vs Full QoE

Not every deal justifies a Big 4-grade Quality of Earnings report. The question is matching diligence depth to deal size, data maturity, and decision risk.

Full QoE

A full QoE is an independent, assurance-style analysis: detailed revenue recognition testing, cost allocations, add-back verification, NWC normalization, and extensive corroboration against ledgers and third-party evidence. It suits larger transactions, institutional lenders, and contested processes where the report itself becomes part of the financing package.

Light QoE

A light QoE focuses on the highest-risk areas for smaller deals:

  • Revenue recognition — is booked revenue real, complete, and correctly timed?
  • Cost allocations — do expenses land where management says they do?
  • Customer concentration — how exposed is EBITDA to the top few customers?
  • Add-back sanity check — which adjustments would survive buyer scrutiny?

Frequently asked questions

What is a light QoE?

A focused review covering the core adjustments: owner remuneration, obvious non-recurring items, headline revenue quality and a working capital overview. It suits smaller deals where the analytical budget is limited and the buyer's own team will do the deeper work.

What is a full QoE?

A complete quality of earnings: contract-level revenue testing, detailed cost analysis, working capital seasonality, standalone cost modelling, and a full net debt and debt-like items review. It suits mid-market transactions where the buyer is relying on the pack rather than building their own.

How do you decide which scope to recommend?

We look at three things: the size of the transaction, the sophistication of the buyer's team, and the complexity of the target's accounting. If the buyer has a strong team and the target is straightforward, a light QoE is usually sufficient. If the buyer is relying on the pack and the target has related-party transactions or unusual cost structures, we recommend full scope.
